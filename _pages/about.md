I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science / AI at [Open Universiteit](https://www.ou.nl/), Heerlen, Netherlands, where I co-developed the MSc course Software Engineering and AI and teach Time Series Analysis and Forecasting.

My research centers on machine learning for time series — occupancy inference and energy optimization from sparse IoT sensor data (RNNs, Transformers, graph neural networks) — and on multi-agent systems for peer-to-peer energy trading, including the design of blockchain-based smart contracts for battery storage markets.

I also work on the societal effects of AI — particularly how generative AI has scaled disinformation faster than our ability to evaluate it. Beyond detecting AI-generated content, I'm interested in identifying content engineered to trigger emotional, viral sharing, and in interventions that introduce friction at the moment of sharing — a prompt asking "verify first?" when content shows signs of manipulation, for instance — grounded in NLP methods like supervised classifiers and evidence-aware text generation. Since the people most likely to share viral content are also the least likely to adopt such tools voluntarily, I think this kind of intervention belongs embedded in institutions and AI-literacy curricula, not left to individual choice.

Before moving into computer science, I trained as a geophysicist (PhD, Utrecht University), working on GNSS network analysis, earthquake hazard modeling, and Earth observation. That background still shapes how I think about probabilistic modeling and messy, real-world sensor data.

A full list of positions, teaching, and publications is on my [CV](/cv/), [Teaching](/teaching/), and [Publications](/publications/) pages.
